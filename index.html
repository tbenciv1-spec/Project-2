Day-5 Flag Review Tool

For a DSNY supervisor deciding whether to review Missed Collection requests that are still open after N days. Built from NYC 311 data (Mar 9 to May 29, 2026). Historical replay, not live data.

Try it: change the three boxes in Section 1, then read the results in Section 2. Green means your team could handle the flagged requests. Red means they could not.

1. Set the flag rule

Flag a request once it has been open this many days.

Count a request as late if it took longer than this many days. This is only used to score the rule.

The most flagged requests your team can review in one day. You choose this number. It is your guess about your team's capacity, not something the data tells us.

Change one box at a time so you can see what each one does.

2. What the rule would have flagged

This is your three settings above, replayed on past requests.

  • Requests flagged: how many requests this rule would have flagged in total.
  • Flagged per day: that total divided by the 82 days. Compare this number with your cap.
  • Flags truly late: of the flagged requests, the share that really took longer than your late line.
  • Late requests caught: of all late requests, the share this rule flagged.
  • Red or green: red means this rule flags more per day than your cap. Try a larger N, or ask whether your cap is realistic.

Which rule fits? One way to choose: pick the rule that flags as many late requests as possible while staying green.

About this tool

  • Data: NYC Open Data, 311 Service Requests from 2020 to Present, agency DSNY, complaint type Missed Collection; 8,887 unique requests created Mar 9 to May 29, 2026. Only the request number and dates are used.
  • Limits: every request is already closed, so this is a replay of what a flag would have caught. Closed does not prove collected. Results describe this dataset only.
  • Review outcomes are saved only in your browser and never sent anywhere. Export the CSV to keep them.
MIS 581S Project 2. Course project, not an official DSNY or City of New York tool.
